Author bio, short

Bob Chapek spent 29 years at The Walt Disney Company, leading its home entertainment, consumer products and parks businesses before serving as Chief Executive Officer from February 2020 to November 2022. He is a graduate of Indiana University and holds an MBA from Michigan State University. He lives in Westlake Village, California, with his wife Cynthia.

Author bio, long

Robert A. Chapek grew up in Robertsdale, in Hammond, Indiana, the son of a refinery machinist and an insurance agent. He studied microbiology at Indiana University, then spent a decade in packaged goods and advertising before joining Disney in 1993 as a marketing director in the home video division, an era when the product shipped on cassette.

He went on to lead Buena Vista Home Entertainment through the move to DVD and Blu-ray. Disney Consumer Products came next, where he folded Star Wars into the company's licensing engine after the Lucasfilm acquisition, making Disney the largest licensor of intellectual property in the world. As chairman of Walt Disney Parks and Resorts he opened Shanghai Disneyland, built Pandora and Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge, and invested more than $24 billion in parks, attractions, hotels and ships.

He became CEO on February 25, 2020, days before the pandemic closed the company's parks and cruise lines. He oversaw the reopening of every site worldwide and the launch of Disney+ as its own business. He left the company in November 2022, and has spent the years since teaching and advising.

Interview angles

  • Crisis leadership: the first seventy-two hours of the pandemic, when most of the company's operating businesses had to close.
  • Succession and its costs: being named the hand-picked successor, and the weekend the board reversed it.
  • Running an experience business at scale: 150 million annual visits, and what that taught him about capacity, queues and trust.
  • A scientist running a studio: how a microbiology degree shaped the way he tests what he asserts.
  • Careers that look illegible early: a videotape marketer who ended up in the corner office.
