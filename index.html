About Bob

From a refinery town to the corner office

Robert A. Chapek grew up in Robertsdale, the Hammond, Indiana neighborhood beside the Whiting oil refinery where his father worked as a machinist. He was an altar boy at St. John the Baptist and graduated from George Rogers Clark High School in 1977, as every member of his family had.

He arrived at Indiana University behind his classmates and spent a year catching up on his own, which he has called his first lesson in grit. He met his wife Cynthia on his second day on campus and watched Breaking Away being filmed around him, his first glimpse of how a production gets made. A microbiology degree took him to brand management at H.J. Heinz, then to advertising, and in 1993 to a Disney division that sold films on cassette.

Twenty-nine years later he had led home entertainment, consumer products and the parks, and he had run the company. He and Cynthia married in 1980, have three children including Marvel Studios producer Brian Chapek, and live in Westlake Village, California.

DOCTRINE

Pick a North Star

He sets one highest value and steers by it when the inputs conflict. In the parks it was exceeding guest expectations, and it made a hundred impossible decisions merely difficult.

METHOD

A scientist's mind

One of very few entertainment CEOs with a microbiology degree. He credits an arts-and-sciences education with the elasticity a media career demands, and reasons by hypothesis, data, iterate.

FORMAT

Three platform deaths

He carried Disney's home entertainment business from tape to DVD to Blu-ray, then into streaming. He has personally led a business through three format transitions.

ROOTS

Guest first

His family drove to Walt Disney World every year when he was a boy. He did not arrive as a strategist studying a brand. He arrived as someone who had stood in its queues.

LICENSING

The world's largest licensor

As head of consumer products he folded Star Wars into Disney's licensing engine after the Lucasfilm deal, making Disney the largest licensor of intellectual property on earth.

SERVICE

A park as public health

Disneyland became one of California's largest vaccination sites. Moving enormous numbers of people through a process is literally the business, so it was the right thing to do and something they knew how to do.

Career

A ladder almost nobody else has climbed

Home video, then consumer products, then parks, then the corner office. Open any milestone.

1977

George Rogers Clark High School, Hammond, Indiana

Roots+

He grew up in Robertsdale, in the shadow of the Whiting refinery his father worked in. His mother ran an insurance agency in East Chicago. His own summary of the place: you get what you earn, and you expect nothing beyond what you work hard for.

1981

B.S. in Microbiology, Indiana University Bloomington

Education+

He arrived under-prepared and caught up on his own, which he has called his first lesson in grit. He met Cynthia on his second day on campus and watched Breaking Away being filmed nearby. An MBA at Michigan State followed, then brand management at H.J. Heinz and advertising at J. Walter Thompson.

1993

Marketing director, Buena Vista Home Entertainment

Entry+

The VHS era. Michael Eisner later said of him: "He was always an executive that you knew would be on the rise." He added that Chapek knew how to grow the business while adjusting to a changing marketplace, which was intense.

2006 to 2009

President of home entertainment, then president of distribution, Walt Disney Studios

Format shift+

He carried the business from tape to DVD to Blu-ray, the sort of transition that has ended other companies. His rise from home video to the top of Disney has been called the home entertainment industry's single biggest success story.

2011

President, Disney Consumer Products

Licensing+

After the Lucasfilm acquisition he folded Star Wars into Disney's licensing machine. In 2013 he closed a Hasbro deal worth $80M in Marvel royalties plus up to $225M for Star Wars rights. In 2014 he launched Disney Imagicademy, the company's first serious move into children's learning apps, prompted by parents telling his team that good ones were impossible to find.

2015

Chairman, Walt Disney Parks and Resorts

Builder+

Handed the biggest capital budget in entertainment. Over his tenure he invested more than $24 billion into parks, attractions, hotels and ships, more, the New York Times noted, than Disney paid for Pixar, Marvel and Lucasfilm combined.

2016

Shanghai Disneyland opens: $5.5B, 11M guests in year one

Landmark+

The project that made his reputation with the board. Bob Iger travelled to Shanghai with him more than ten times, watching how he handled budget overruns and construction delays. It opened. It worked.

2017 to 2019

Pandora and Galaxy's Edge: two fully built worlds

Immersion+

Two of the most ambitious themed lands ever built. Disney called Galaxy's Edge the most immersive land it had ever built, at roughly $1 billion for 14 acres in Anaheim. In 2018 he was given back consumer products and created Disney Parks, Experiences and Products, the clearest signal yet that he was next in line.

Feb 2020

Chief Executive Officer, The Walt Disney Company

The job+

Named by Bob Iger as his hand-picked successor. Three days in, COVID-19 forced his first major decision: shut close to 80% of the company's operating businesses, a figure he has cited himself in interviews, per his 2023 talk at Indiana University. "That was very difficult, because that was something that was unprecedented, unheard of."

2020 to 2021

Reopening the parks, and turning Disneyland into a vaccination site

Crisis+

Shanghai reopened first, capped at 24,000 guests a day, which he called a baby step. Walt Disney World followed with masks and temperature checks. Disneyland became a mass vaccination site, a decision he framed plainly: "If there's one thing we do, it's handle large numbers of people. It was just the right thing to do." Meanwhile Mulan and Soul went straight to Disney+, and the streaming era began in earnest.

2022

The hardest year: Florida, the reversal, the apology

Turbulence+

Chapek initially declined to take a public position on Florida's Parental Rights in Education Act, arguing in a board-approved memo that corporate statements do very little to change outcomes and can be counterproductive. The backlash from Disney's own creative community was severe. He reversed course, apologized, paused Florida political donations and pledged support to LGBTQ+ organizations, but the episode set off the Reedy Creek fight and defined the year.

Nov 20, 2022

Out, five months after a three-year extension

The exit+

A weekend. A phone call. Iger back in the chair. CNBC later reported that Iger had repeatedly failed to champion the successor he chose, keeping his office and calling himself Big Bob. The board pointed to the November earnings report. Chapek has spent four years deciding what he thinks happened, and the answer is the book.

2024 to 2025

Board of directors, Masimo

Next chapter+

He joined the board of the medical technology company in January 2024, during one of the most closely watched governance fights in med-tech, and stepped off in 2025.
